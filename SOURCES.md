# Sources, field meanings and accepted design choices

Read with PROCEDURE.md. This file is the reference both the proposer and the verifier work from. Changes to it are system changes and need Andrzej's merge.

## Fields in country-check-data.json
- `countries` (keyed by map country name): `n` display name; `cpi` / `prev` World Bank Control of Corruption governance score 0-100, latest and prior year (the name "cpi" is historical; it is NOT Transparency International); `fatf` "black" or "grey" (absent = not listed); `fto` "y" identified, "n" none identified, "a" or absent = "Not covered"; `ftog` FTO group names; `us` "c" comprehensive U.S. sanctions, "t" targeted country program; `usp` [program name, OFAC URL]; `eu` EU country-based restrictive measures, including EU implementations of UN regimes, as [regime name, EU Sanctions Map regime page https://www.sanctionsmap.eu/#/main/details/<id>/]; `un` UN Security Council regimes as [name, committee page https://main.un.org/securitycouncil/en/sanctions/<resolution>] (the Lebanon arms embargo under resolution 1701 has no committee and links to the resolution in the UN Digital Library); `uk`, `ca`, `ch`, `au` lists of [name, official URL] (UK regimes including the UK arms embargo on mainland China and Hong Kong; Canadian regulations under SEMA and the UN Act; Swiss ordinances under the Embargo Act, English titles are Paragon's renderings of the German titles, linked to Fedlex; Australian sanctions frameworks, DFAT); `iso2` / `iso3` codes.
- `fto_listings`: the U.S.-designated FTO groups (State Department, INA section 219), each with an empty list. Since 27 October 2026 the FTO view is U.S.-only (Andrzej's decision): other governments' listings of these groups are never added, shown or checked, and `validate.py` rejects any.
- `fatf_evaluations`: latest FATF mutual evaluation per jurisdiction, [evaluation date YYYY-MM, latest ratings update YYYY-MM, assessment body]. The page shows only date and body, never ratings (licensing).
- `crt_links`: map country name to the slug of its page in the State Department's Country Reports on Terrorism 2024 (https://www.state.gov/reports/country-reports-on-terrorism-2024/<slug>/).
- `meta.page`: the page's as-of text and each view's "checked" date (see PROCEDURE.md).

## Where to check (for sanctions, read each government's thematic headings as well as its country list: standing decision 14. the cloud shell reaches the World Bank API, gov.uk, publicsafety.gc.ca, nationalsecurity.gov.au, legislation.gov.au, the UN XML, the EU financial sanctions XML, the EU Sanctions Map API, the Federal Register API and OpenSanctions. fatf-gafi.org and, at times, state.gov refuse the shell and web fetch: read them through the "Fetch sources" workflow (PROCEDURE.md). dfat.gov.au refuses every server: use the Federal Register of Legislation instead)
- **U.S. FTO designations**: Federal Register API is primary (documents with agency state-department and term "Foreign Terrorist Organization" since the last check, plus https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/public-inspection-documents/current.json); also https://www.state.gov/foreign-terrorist-organizations . Watch alternate names (Ansarallah = the Houthis). For a newly designated group, presence in a country comes only from State Department reporting (designation notice, Country Reports, "Areas of Operation"); every such change is strict.
- **State Department country report links**: the Country Reports on Terrorism 2024 index https://www.state.gov/reports/country-reports-on-terrorism-2024/ ; report to Andrzej if a 2025 edition is published (the FTO view and links would need rework, which is a system change).
- **OFAC**: https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-programs-and-country-information (comprehensive embargoes currently Cuba, Iran, North Korea, plus the occupied Ukraine regions note).
- **UK**: https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/uk-sanctions-regimes-under-the-sanctions-act
- **Canada**: https://www.international.gc.ca/world-monde/international_relations-relations_internationales/sanctions/current-actuelles.aspx?lang=eng , including its issue-based entries ("Terrorism" covers the Taliban regulations, SOR/99-444, which count for Afghanistan; standing decision 14). Regulations are cited and quoted from Justice Laws (laws-lois.justice.gc.ca).
- **Switzerland**: https://www.seco.admin.ch/de/sanktionsverordnungen (German); Fedlex links.
- **Australia**: new or ended frameworks are confirmed against the Federal Register of Legislation (https://www.legislation.gov.au : the Autonomous Sanctions Regulations 2011 and the Charter of the United Nations sanctions regulations for the country concerned); quote and cite the legislation page. The page's au links stay on DFAT (https://www.dfat.gov.au/international-relations/security/sanctions/sanctions-regimes-currently-implemented-under-australian-sanction-law ; every au link points to its own framework page under /sanctions/sanctions-regimes/), which no server can read; the link check's "unreachable" lines for dfat.gov.au are expected.
- **EU**: EU Sanctions Map (https://www.sanctionsmap.eu , API /api/v1/regime). Include EU regimes implementing UN regimes; exclude the legacy "prohibiting the satisfying of certain claims" regimes. Each EU link uses the regime id from the API; the source check confirms the id matches the regime name.
- **UN Security Council regimes**: https://main.un.org/securitycouncil/en/sanctions/information
- **FATF lists**: https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/countries/black-and-grey-lists.html and the plenary outcome publications, read through the "Fetch sources" workflow. List changes are strict (two sources: the outcomes publication and the list publication). FinCEN's post-Plenary release (fincen.gov) is a useful extra check. After applying a Plenary's changes, replace the FATF entry in `meta.next_events` with the next Plenary as FATF announces it.
- **FATF mutual evaluations**: the consolidated assessment ratings tables (FATF blocks cloud downloads; a monthly task on Andrzej's computer reads them) and mutual evaluation reports published since.
- **World Bank WGI**: only when source 3's lastupdated (https://api.worldbank.org/v2/sources?format=json) is later than the date in meta.as_of.cpi; compare GOV_WGI_CC.SC rounded, by iso3.
- **Outside alarm, never a source (standing decision 11)**: OpenSanctions https://data.opensanctions.org/datasets/latest/us_state_terrorist_orgs/targets.simple.csv . It lags new State Department designations by weeks; when the source check reports that State's list matches the file, an OpenSanctions count gap is expected and needs no action.

## Accepted design choices (not errors; do not propose changes)
Israel and Palestine share one FTO entry and one State Department link; Taiwan is scored; Croatia and Slovenia sit under OFAC's Balkans program; the Basel AML Index and Transparency International's CPI are deliberately not shown; FATF ratings are deliberately not reproduced; U.S. territories and similar dependencies without their own FATF evaluation show "no published FATF evaluation"; Australia's former Federal Republic of Yugoslavia framework and the EU "satisfying of claims" regimes are deliberately not mapped to a country; sanctions regimes with worldwide scope (ISIL and Al-Qaida, counter-terrorism lists, global human rights, anti-corruption, cyber and proliferation regimes) are excluded, but a measure aimed at one country or its government or de facto authorities counts for that country whatever heading it is filed under (standing decision 14; for example Canada's Taliban regulations, listed under "Terrorism", count for Afghanistan); the U.S. Ethiopia-related program ended in September 2026; Cuba, Iran and Syria have no Country Reports on Terrorism country page (State Sponsors chapter), so no State Department link shows for them. The Country Reports on Terrorism 2024 pages for Argentina, Colombia and Cote d'Ivoire return 404 because the State Department unpublished them (search indexes show Argentina and Colombia under "__trashed" addresses), while State's own report index still links to the same addresses we use (checked 29 October 2026). The source check's 404 warnings for these three links are expected: keep the links, do not raise them with Andrzej, and report only if State publishes them at a different address or drops them from the report index.
